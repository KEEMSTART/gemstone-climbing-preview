Gemstone Adaptive Climbing Program

everyBODY can climb.

The first fully ADA-accessible climbing gym in the world — and a program built to prove the walls are for everyone.

Our mission

To empower individuals of all abilities by building self-esteem, confidence and lifetime skills through climbing, yoga and fitness.

We strive to bring a sense of community to those facing challenges — to give them purpose and a place to belong. We adapt to meet everyone’s needs, not just abilities, creating opportunities that foster inclusivity, build confidence, and inspire a lifelong love of adventure.

World’s first fully ADA-accessible climbing gym Program launched October 2019 All equipment provided

About the program

Gemstone was the first fully ADA-accessible climbing gym in the world. Today other gyms are building similar facilities — and we applaud them.

Clinics, not just sessions

Participants with physical, cognitive, and social disabilities work alongside an expanding volunteer base of medical and climbing professionals.

Breaking down barriers

Our climbers get the chance to break down barriers and become adventurers — experiencing the sensation of being on top of the world.

Personal goals

Through interactive, engaging clinics, challenged athletes grow as individuals and build the confidence to reach their own fitness goals.

Who the program serves

Some of the most advanced and effective “therapy” available — for individuals living with:

Autism Cerebral Palsy Traumatic Brain Injury Multiple Sclerosis Spina Bifida Hearing & Vision Impairments PTSD Paralysis Amputations

We adapt to meet everyone’s needs — this list isn’t a limit. Reach out and we’ll figure it out together.

Why climbing works

Climbing is a full-body workout, from your fingertips to your toes — and the benefits reach well past the wall.

Physical

Builds strength and fine and gross motor skills, while developing the vestibular system (balance) and proprioception (spatial awareness) through tactile, visually stimulating movement.

  • Practices eye–hand and eye–foot coordination
  • Bilateral coordination of upper and lower extremities
  • Range-of-motion work increases flexion and extension, helping prevent muscle atrophy
  • At any physical level, body movement increases circulation and assists digestion

Cognitive

Every route is a puzzle — climbers solve it by choosing hand and foot placements all the way to the top.

  • Decision-making: choosing a path and following through
  • Scanning the wall for the next hold removes distractions and increases attention
  • Helps children think sequentially
  • Allows independent movement — well suited to climbers with autism who may not like physical contact
  • Cross-curricular play: finding letters or spelling words on the wall
  • Receptive language: processing verbal direction, like which colored hold to reach for
  • Climbers focus better on cognitive tasks after whole-body activity

Social

Climbing is a social sport — it takes at least two people to do it.

  • The Magic Valley climbing community keeps growing
  • More resources than ever for individuals of all abilities to find programs and climbers like themselves
  • A built-in place to belong

Emotional

Climbing promotes positive mood and mental well-being while combating boredom, anxiety, and stress.

  • Increases self-esteem
  • Builds positive coping skills
  • Creates a sense of purpose and accomplishment
